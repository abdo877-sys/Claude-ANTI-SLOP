# Visual research — principles, not copies

Optional, time-boxed (≤15 minutes of browsing), and only when a browser or
web tools are available. Skip it for small changes.

## Where to look
Awwwards, SiteInspire, Godly, Minimal Gallery, Land-book, Httpster, Framer
showcase, Typewolf (type in use), Fonts In Use, and independent portfolios.
For product UI: the real products in the user's category that people praise.
**Also look outside the web:** the product's physical world (signage, packaging,
print, tools, uniforms) — it's where the metaphor comes from.

## Protocol
1. Search for the **problem**, not the style: "pricing page for usage-based B2B",
   "portfolio for a motion designer", "docs site for a CLI".
2. Collect 5–8 references. For each, write **one principle** it demonstrates:
   - "Leads with the product's real output, not a description of it."
   - "Uses a single condensed face at extreme sizes; everything else is small."
   - "Navigation is the timeline; no separate menu."
3. Discard references whose principle doesn't fit the brief's evidence.
4. Combine 2–3 principles into your own composition. If a reader could
   name the site you took it from, you copied — change it.

## Never
- Reproduce a site's layout, copy, illustrations, photos, icons or code.
- Use another brand's colors + type combination together.
- Download assets from galleries into the project.
- Claim the result is "Awwwards-level" or "award-winning".

## Recording
Put the principles (not screenshots of other sites) in the creative brief
under "References → principles", with the source URL for each.
